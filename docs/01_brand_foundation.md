# Brand Foundation

## Brand Idea

**Voxa** is a voice training app designed especially for trans* people. The product should feel supportive, respectful and empowering. It is not about “fixing” a voice. It is about helping users shape, explore and train their voice intentionally.

## Core Promise

Voxa helps users understand their voice, practice with confidence and track progress over time.

## Brand Values

- **Self-determined** – the user decides what voice goals matter
- **Affirming** – feedback supports growth without shame
- **Precise** – training data should be understandable and useful
- **Calm** – the interface should reduce pressure and overload
- **Inclusive** – the brand should not be narrowly gender-coded

## Positioning

Voxa sits between a technical training tool and a supportive wellbeing product.

It should feel:
- more precise than a generic meditation or wellness app
- warmer and more human than a cold measurement tool
- more inclusive than a product aimed only at one binary transition path

## Audience

Primary audience:
- trans women
- trans men
- non-binary people
- gender-questioning users who want to explore voice presentation

Secondary audience:
- speech therapy adjacent self-training users
- users who want structured pitch practice and progress tracking

## Brand Personality

If Voxa were a person, it would feel:
- calm
- competent
- modern
- discreet
- warm
- encouraging

It should not feel:
- judgmental
- sterile
- hyper-feminine
- hyper-masculine
- loud or gamified in an exhausting way

## Naming Rationale

**Voxa** is short, international and easy to pronounce. It suggests voice without sounding clinical or overly technical.

Likely pronunciation:
- German: **VOK-sa**
- English: **VOK-suh**

## Messaging Direction

Good themes:
- finding your voice
- practicing with confidence
- shaping your voice your way
- progress without pressure

Example lines:
- **Find your voice.**
- **Train your voice, your way.**
- **Finde deine Stimme.**
- **Deine Stimme. Dein Weg.**

## Content Tone

The product copy should be:
- affirming
- clear
- calm
- direct
- non-judgmental

Prefer:
- “You are close to your target range.”
- “Try to keep the pitch a little more stable.”
- “That was a strong practice session.”

Avoid:
- “wrong”
- “not good enough”
- “male enough / female enough”
- shaming or overly corrective wording
